# Research Angle 5: 3D Printing Materials — Complete Guide

## Executive Summary

This guide covers all major 3D printing materials available for FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) and SLA (Stereolithography) printers, with detailed properties, pricing, and application guidance. The focus is on practical, actionable information for users in Iraq, including local availability and import considerations. FDM filament prices globally range from $10–$60/kg depending on material and brand, with an estimated 30–60% markup for imports into Iraq.

PLA remains the dominant material globally (~60% of all filament sales) due to its ease of printing, low cost ($15–25/kg), and acceptable properties for decorative and light-functional parts. PETG has emerged as the best balance of printability and engineering performance for intermediate users, while ABS, Nylon, and Polycarbonate serve specialized high-temperature or high-strength applications. Resin (SLA) materials offer superior surface finish and detail at the cost of brittleness, post-processing requirements, and safety precautions. For Iraq, PLA and PETG are the most readily available materials through local suppliers like Erbil Plastic, Global Iraq, and Creality Iraq, with prices ranging from 25,000–45,000 IQD/kg.

## Detailed Material Profiles

### PLA (Polylactic Acid)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: ~37 MPa (PLA+) up to ~60 MPa
- Flexural Strength: ~60 MPa
- Elongation at Break: ~6% (brittle)
- Heat Deflection Temperature (HDT): 55–60°C
- UV Resistance: Poor — degrades in direct sunlight
- Layer Adhesion: Very good (for FDM)
- Biodegradable (industrial composting conditions)

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 190–220°C (standard), 210–230°C (PLA+)
- Bed Temp: 40–60°C (heated bed optional)
- Enclosure: Not required (open frame fine)
- Cooling: 100% fan recommended
- Print Speed: 40–150 mm/s (high-speed variants up to 300+ mm/s)

**Print Difficulty: Easy.**
The most beginner-friendly material. Low warp, no enclosure needed, forgiving of imperfect settings. PLA+ (or Pro) variants add impact resistance while retaining easy printability.

**Common Uses:**
- Visual prototypes and concept models
- Decorative items, toys, figurines
- Trade show displays
- Fixtures and jigs (low load)
- Educational projects
- Cosmetic parts

**Price:**
- Budget: $13–18/kg (Sunlu, eSun on sale)
- Mid-range: $20–25/kg (Hatchbox, Polymaker, Overture)
- Premium: $28–35/kg (Prusament, Bambu Lab Matte)
- Iraq (local): 25,000–40,000 IQD/kg
- Iraq (import shipped): 30,000–50,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Sanding: Easy (sands well)
- Painting: Excellent (primer recommended)
- Gluing: Good (CA glue, epoxy)
- Acetone vapor: Does NOT work (try ethyl acetate for smoothing)
- Drilling/tapping: Good

**Pros:** Easy to print, wide color range, no fumes, low cost, biodegradable base, great surface finish, vast community support.

**Cons:** Brittle, low heat resistance, degrades in UV, not for mechanical loads, poor chemical resistance.

**Safety:** Generally safe — emits mostly lactide (mild irritant). Low VOC/particulate compared to ABS. Still, print in ventilated area.

**Storage:** Low moisture sensitivity. Store in sealed bag with desiccant. Dry at 45–50°C for 4–6 hours if needed.

### ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: ~27–40 MPa
- Flexural Strength: ~60–70 MPa
- Elongation at Break: 3.5–50% (varies by formulation)
- HDT: ~100°C
- UV Resistance: Poor — degrades in sunlight
- Layer Adhesion: Good (with proper enclosure)
- Impact Resistance: Very good (much tougher than PLA)

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 230–250°C
- Bed Temp: 90–110°C
- Enclosure: Required (chamber temp 40–60°C recommended)
- Cooling: 0–30% fan (minimal cooling)
- Known for warping — use brim/raft, enclosure, and gradual cooling

**Print Difficulty: Hard.**
High shrinkage during cooling causes edge lifting (warping). Requires a heated enclosure. Stringing and layer adhesion issues common. Acetone vapor smoothing is a key advantage.

**Common Uses:**
- Functional prototypes
- Automotive interior parts
- Power tool housings
- Electronics enclosures
- LEGO-like parts
- Parts requiring acetone smoothing

**Price:**
- Budget: $15–20/kg
- Mid-range: $20–27/kg
- Premium: $28–35/kg
- Iraq (local): 25,000–35,000 IQD/kg
- Iraq (import): 35,000–50,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Acetone vapor smoothing: Excellent — produces glossy, injection-mold-like finish
- Sanding: Good (sands well, wet sanding recommended)
- Painting: Excellent (acetone prime first)
- Gluing: Good with acetone welding or epoxy
- Drilling/tapping: Excellent

**Pros:** Tough, impact-resistant, high temp resistance (100°C), acetone-smoothable, good for functional parts, LEGO-compatible.

**Cons:** Hard to print (warping), enclosure mandatory, strong fumes (styrene), poor UV resistance, not food-safe, requires venting.

**Safety:** WARNING — ABS emits styrene (classified as possible carcinogen), high UFPs, and VOCs. An enclosure with activated carbon filtration and exhaust to outside is highly recommended. Never print ABS in a bedroom or unventilated space.

**Storage:** Moderate moisture sensitivity. Dry at 65–70°C for 4–6 hours. Store sealed with desiccant.

### PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: ~50 MPa
- Flexural Strength: ~65 MPa
- Elongation at Break: ~15–50%
- HDT: ~80°C
- UV Resistance: Good (better than PLA and ABS)
- Layer Adhesion: Excellent (among the best for FDM)
- Chemical Resistance: Good (resists acids, alcohols)

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 230–250°C
- Bed Temp: 70–80°C
- Enclosure: Not required but helps with consistency
- Cooling: 30–50% fan (excessive cooling weakens layer adhesion)
- Bed adhesion: Glue stick or PEI sheet (PETG can damage bare glass)

**Print Difficulty: Moderate.**
Easier than ABS, harder than PLA. Requires careful bed adhesion setup and retraction tuning to control stringing. Prone to oozing. Forgiving once dialed in.

**Common Uses:**
- Functional mechanical parts
- Outdoor items (planters, signs)
- Food containers (food-safe grade)
- Medical applications
- Snap-fit components
- Protective guards and covers

**Price:**
- Budget: $18–22/kg
- Mid-range: $22–28/kg
- Premium: $28–35/kg
- Iraq (local): 28,000–45,000 IQD/kg
- Iraq (import): 35,000–55,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Sanding: Good (not as easy as PLA but sands well)
- Painting: Good (but needs primer)
- Gluing: Fair (harder to glue than PLA/ABS — use specialized adhesives like Weld-On)
- Clear coat: Can achieve glass-like clarity with clear PETG

**Pros:** Stronger and more durable than PLA, better temp resistance, good UV resistance, prints without enclosure, food-safe variants available, excellent layer adhesion, good chemical resistance.

**Cons:** Stringing/oozing issues, can damage bare glass beds, harder to post-process (gluing), slightly more expensive than PLA, absorbs moisture rapidly.

**Safety:** Lower emissions than ABS — moderate VOC levels. Print in ventilated area. Food-safe when printed with stainless steel nozzle and proper care.

**Storage:** Moderate–high moisture sensitivity. Dry at 65°C for 6–8 hours before printing if exposed to air for >1 week. Store in dry box.

### TPU / TPE (Flexible Filament)

**Properties:**
- Shore Hardness: 85A–98A (TPU), softer for TPE
- Tensile Strength: 25–35 MPa
- Elongation at Break: 300–600% (extremely stretchy)
- HDT: ~80°C
- UV Resistance: Good
- Abrasion Resistance: Excellent
- Layer Adhesion: Good

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 210–240°C
- Bed Temp: 40–60°C
- Enclosure: Not required
- Cooling: 30–50% fan
- Print Speed: 15–40 mm/s (slow)
- Extruder: Direct drive strongly recommended (Bowden is difficult)
- Retraction: Minimal or disabled (reduces clogging)

**Print Difficulty: Moderate.**
Requires slow speeds and direct-drive extruder. Bowden setups struggle with flexible filaments. Stringing common. Oozing can be an issue.

**Common Uses:**
- Phone cases
- Gaskets and seals
- Vibration dampening feet
- Wearable devices
- RC car tires
- Flexible hinges
- Prosthetic sockets
- Shock-absorbing parts

**Price:**
- Budget: $20–30/kg
- Mid-range: $30–40/kg
- Premium: $40–55/kg
- Iraq (local): 35,000–55,000 IQD/kg
- Iraq (import): 45,000–70,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Sanding: Difficult (squishy surface)
- Painting: Challenging (needs flexible primer)
- Gluing: Special adhesives needed (cyanoacrylate works poorly)
- Dyeing: Possible with specific techniques

**Pros:** Excellent flexibility and elasticity, high impact resistance, good abrasion resistance, durable, good chemical resistance.

**Cons:** Slow print speed, requires direct drive, stringing/oozing, poor bridging, more expensive, harder to post-process.

**Safety:** Low-moderate emissions. Print in ventilated area. Some TPU grades emit siloxanes.

**Storage:** High moisture sensitivity. Dry at 55°C for 5–8 hours. Must be stored dry — TPU absorbs moisture quickly, causing bubbling.

### Nylon (PA6 / PA12 / PA-CF)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: 50–85 MPa
- Flexural Strength: 60–90 MPa
- Elongation at Break: 20–300% (PA12 more flexible)
- HDT: 180°C+ (annealed can exceed 200°C)
- UV Resistance: Good
- Chemical Resistance: Excellent (oils, solvents, fuels)
- Abrasion Resistance: Excellent (self-lubricating)
- Layer Adhesion: Good (but moisture-sensitive)

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 240–280°C (needs all-metal hotend)
- Bed Temp: 70–100°C
- Enclosure: Required (chamber temp 50–60°C)
- Cooling: 0–30% fan
- Nozzle: Hardened steel for PA-CF (abrasive)

**Print Difficulty: Hard.**
Extremely moisture sensitive — wet nylon prints with bubbling, stringing, and weak layers. Requires enclosure, all-metal hotend, and meticulous drying. PA12 is somewhat easier than PA6. Carbon-fiber variants are stiffer but require hardened nozzles.

**Common Uses:**
- Gears and mechanical parts
- Bearings and bushings
- Functional prototypes
- Automotive under-hood parts
- Durable enclosures
- Tool handles
- Structural components

**Price:**
- PA6/PA12: $30–50/kg
- PA-CF: $40–65/kg
- Iraq: Very limited local availability, mostly import via Amazon/aliexpress
- Iraq (import): 50,000–90,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Sanding: Good
- Painting: Requires primer
- Gluing: Epoxy or CA glue works
- Dyeing: Excellent — nylon absorbs fabric dyes (can be dyed after printing)
- Annealing: Improves strength and temp resistance

**Pros:** Extremely strong and durable, high temperature resistance, excellent chemical/oil resistance, self-lubricating, can be dyed, great for gears.

**Cons:** Very hygroscopic (must be bone dry), requires enclosure, all-metal hotend, expensive, challenging to print, warps easily, bed adhesion is tricky.

**Safety:** Nylon PA6 emits caprolactam (mild irritant). Moderate VOC emissions. Use enclosure and ventilation.

**Storage:** VERY HIGH moisture sensitivity. Must be dried at 70–80°C for 8–12 hours before printing. Store in vacuum-sealed bags with heavy desiccant. Nylon left out for 2 hours in humid air will degrade print quality.

### Polycarbonate (PC)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: ~65–70 MPa
- Flexural Strength: ~90 MPa
- Elongation at Break: ~10–30%
- HDT: 120–140°C (higher with annealing)
- UV Resistance: Good (with UV-stabilized grades)
- Impact Resistance: Extremely high (near-unbreakable)
- Transparency: Excellent optical clarity possible
- Layer Adhesion: Good with proper settings

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 260–310°C (requires all-metal hotend)
- Bed Temp: 100–130°C
- Enclosure: Required (chamber temp 60–70°C)
- Cooling: 0–20% fan (minimal)
- Bed adhesion: Polyimide tape, PEI, or hairspray
- Nozzle: Hardened steel recommended

**Print Difficulty: Expert.**
One of the most challenging FDM materials. Very high print temperatures (approaching limits of most printers). Extreme warp tendency. Requires a well-insulated enclosure capable of maintaining high chamber temperatures. Active chamber heating is ideal. Only for advanced users.

**Common Uses:**
- High-strength engineering parts
- Impact-resistant components
- Power tool housings
- RC/propeller parts
- High-temperature applications (near engine)
- Bullet-resistant/security components
- Transparent parts

**Price:**
- $35–60/kg (PC)
- PC-CF/PC blends: $50–75/kg
- Iraq: Rarely available locally, must import
- Iraq (import): 55,000–95,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Sanding: Good
- Painting: Good with primer
- Gluing: Excellent with dichloromethane or epoxy
- Flame polishing: Can restore transparency
- Annealing: Recommended for strength
- Drilling/tapping: Excellent

**Pros:** Extremely tough and impact-resistant, high temperature resistance, excellent strength, can be transparent, good chemical resistance, great mechanical properties.

**Cons:** Very difficult to print, extreme warp tendency, requires high temperatures (300°C+), needs well-heated enclosure, expensive, very hygroscopic.

**Safety:** PC emits BPA when heated (endocrine disruptor concern). Use enclosure with exhaust/HEPA filtration. Not for food use. Ventilate well.

**Storage:** VERY HIGH moisture sensitivity. Dry at 80–90°C for 8–12 hours mandatory before printing. Store vacuum-sealed with desiccant.

### ASA (Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate)

**Properties:**
- Tensile Strength: ~40 MPa
- Flexural Strength: ~65 MPa
- Elongation at Break: ~10–25%
- HDT: ~95–100°C
- UV Resistance: Excellent (best among common FDM filaments)
- Chemical Resistance: Good
- Impact Resistance: Similar to ABS

**Printing Settings:**
- Nozzle Temp: 240–260°C
- Bed Temp: 90–110°C
- Enclosure: Required
- Cooling: 0–30% fan
- Very similar print parameters to ABS but with slightly higher temperature requirements

**Print Difficulty: Hard.**
Very similar to ABS — needs enclosure, suffers from warping. Slightly less forgiving than ABS but produces parts that survive sun exposure for years. The UV stability is the main selling point.

**Common Uses:**
- Outdoor applications (signs, enclosures, trim)
- Automotive exterior (mirror housings, trim)
- Marine parts
- Garden equipment
- Anything exposed to direct sunlight
- Roof and gutter components

**Price:**
- $25–40/kg
- Iraq: Some local availability via specialty suppliers
- Iraq (import): 40,000–60,000 IQD/kg

**Post-Processing:**
- Acetone vapor smoothing: Works (similar to ABS)
- Sanding: Good
- Painting: Excellent
- Gluing: Good with acetone or epoxy

**Pros:** UV stable (years outdoors), similar properties to ABS, smoothable with acetone, good layer adhesion, weather resistant.

**Cons:** Hard to print (enclosure needed), warps, strong fumes (similar to ABS), expensive than ABS, limited color range.

**Safety:** Emits styrene like ABS. Same precautions required: enclosure + ventilation + filtration. Moderate-high emissions.

**Storage:** Moderate moisture sensitivity. Dry at 70°C for 4–6 hours. Store sealed with desiccant.

### Specialty Filaments

#### Wood-Filled PLA
- PLA base with ~30% wood particles (pine, bamboo, birch, etc.)
- **Printing:** 190–220°C, 50–60°C bed, 0.5mm+ nozzle recommended
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Properties:** Wood-like smell, can be stained/sanded, feels like wood
- **Uses:** Decorative items, gift boxes, architectural models, cosplay
- **Price:** $25–40/kg (Iraq: 35,000–55,000 IQD)
- **Caution:** Abrasive (use hardened nozzle), prone to clogging with small nozzles

#### Metal-Filled (Bronze, Copper, Stainless Steel, Iron)
- PLA base with ~60–80% metal powder
- **Printing:** 190–220°C, 60–70°C bed, 0.6mm+ hardened nozzle required
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (heavy, abrasive, needs post-processing)
- **Properties:** Heavy, can be sanded/polished to metallic finish, oxidizes naturally
- **Uses:** Props, jewelry, decorative art, awards
- **Price:** $40–60/kg (Iraq: 55,000–85,000 IQD)
- **Post-processing:** Sand up to high grit → polish with metal polish. Iron can be rusted with vinegar for patina.

#### Carbon Fiber Filled (PA-CF, PETG-CF, PLA-CF)
- Polymer base + chopped carbon fiber
- **Printing:** Follow base polymer temp, hardened nozzle required (very abrasive)
- **Difficulty:** Hard (requires hardened nozzle, enclosure for PA-CF)
- **Properties:** Very high stiffness-to-weight ratio, matte finish, hides layer lines
- **Uses:** Lightweight structural parts, drone frames, automotive, robotics
- **Price:** $35–65/kg (Iraq: 50,000–90,000 IQD)
- **Safety:** Carbon fiber particles are respiratory irritants — wear mask when sanding. Similar to asbestos fiber concerns.

#### Glow-in-the-Dark (PLA-based)
- PLA + phosphorescent pigment (strontium aluminate)
- **Printing:** 200–220°C, use hardened nozzle (abrasive), 0.5mm+ recommended
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Properties:** Glows 2–8 hours after light exposure
- **Uses:** Toys, safety signs, novelty items, keychains
- **Price:** $25–35/kg (Iraq: 35,000–50,000 IQD)

#### Conductive
- PLA/ABS + carbon black or graphene
- **Printing:** Follow base polymer temp, hardened nozzle
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Properties:** Surface resistivity ~100–1000 Ω·cm (NOT a wire replacement — use for touch sensors, low-power circuits)
- **Uses:** Touch-capacitive buttons, simple circuits, ESD-safe parts
- **Price:** $40–70/kg (very niche)

#### Color-Changing (UV/Thermochromic)
- PLA with pigments that change color with UV exposure or temperature
- **Printing:** 190–210°C (lower temp preserves pigment), open frame
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Uses:** Novelty items, toys, temperature indicators, art
- **Price:** $30–45/kg

#### PVA (Polyvinyl Alcohol) — Support Material
- Water-soluble support material
- **Printing:** 190–220°C, 45–60°C bed, direct drive recommended
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Properties:** Dissolves in water (leave 6–24 hours depending on density)
- **Uses:** Complex supports for PLA/PETG, dissolvable interfaces
- **Price:** $60–100/kg (expensive)
- **Storage:** EXTREMELY moisture sensitive — hygroscopic beyond nylon. Must be kept bone dry. Dry at 45°C for 6+ hours.

#### HIPS (High Impact Polystyrene) — Support Material
- Support material primarily for ABS
- **Printing:** 230–250°C, 90–110°C bed, enclosure
- **Properties:** Dissolves in d-limonene (citrus-based solvent)
- **Uses:** Large support structures for ABS prints
- **Price:** $25–40/kg
- **Safety:** D-limonene is a strong solvent — use in ventilated area with gloves.

#### Polypropylene (PP)
- **Printing:** 220–260°C, 85–100°C bed, enclosure ideal
- **Properties:** Semi-flexible, living hinge capable, chemical resistant, low density (floats)
- **Difficulty:** Hard (extremely poor bed adhesion — needs PP-specific build surface or tape)
- **Uses:** Living hinges, chemical containers, battery boxes, food storage
- **Price:** $30–50/kg
- **Storage:** Low moisture sensitivity.

## Resin Materials (SLA/DLP)

### Standard Resin
- **Tensile Strength:** 50–65 MPa
- **Elongation at Break:** 2–6% (brittle)
- **HDT:** 60–75°C
- **Properties:** High detail, smooth surface finish, brittle, UV-degrades
- **Uses:** Miniatures, jewelry prototypes, dental models, figurines
- **Price:** $20–35/L (Iraq: 30,000–55,000 IQD/L)
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (requires washing + UV curing, PPE)

### Tough/Engineering Resin (ABS-Like)
- **Tensile Strength:** 55–65 MPa
- **Elongation at Break:** 15–40%
- **HDT:** 48–90°C
- **Properties:** Impact resistant, less brittle than standard, closer to ABS/PP
- **Uses:** Functional prototypes, snap-fits, housings, jigs
- **Price:** $40–80/L (Iraq: 60,000–120,000 IQD/L)
- **Note:** Formlabs Tough 1000/1500/2000 series (2025/2026) now rival HDPE, PP, and ABS

### Flexible Resin
- **Shore Hardness:** 40A–80A
- **Elongation at Break:** 120–330%
- **Properties:** Rubber-like, elastic, tear-resistant (Formlabs Flexible 80A, Elastic 50A)
- **Uses:** Gaskets, seals, grips, wearables, prosthetic parts
- **Price:** $60–100/L (expensive)

### Castable Resin (for Jewelry)
- **Properties:** Burns out cleanly with ~0% ash residue for investment casting
- **Uses:** Jewelry patterns, dental crowns, small metal parts
- **Price:** $50–100/L
- **Note:** Two types — wax-based for jewelry, ceramic-filled for larger parts
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (needs burnout schedule tuning)

### Dental/Medical Resin
- **Properties:** Biocompatible (ISO 10993 or CE certified), sterilizable, precise
- **Uses:** Surgical guides, orthodontic models, crowns, dentures
- **Price:** $80–200/L (premium, certified)
- **Regulatory:** Requires specific certification for patient contact
- **Availability:** Very limited in Iraq — typically imported by dental clinics

### Water-Washable Resin
- **Properties:** Cleans with water instead of IPA (isopropyl alcohol)
- **Uses:** Convenient for home users, reduces solvent use
- **Price:** $25–45/L
- **Note:** Still need to cure in UV. Wastewater contains uncured resin — do NOT pour down drain. Must be cured and filtered.

## Choosing the Right Material

### Decision Guide by Use Case

| If you want to... | Choose this material | Why |
|-------------------|--------------------|-----|
| Learn 3D printing | PLA | Easiest, cheapest, most forgiving |
| Print functional mechanical parts | PETG or Nylon | Good strength + printability balance |
| Print outdoor parts (sun exposed) | ASA or PETG | UV stable, weather resistant |
| Print car interior parts (hot car) | ABS or ASA | Handles 100°C interior temps |
| Print flexible/gasket parts | TPU | Shore 85A–98A flexibility |
| Print living hinges | PP or Nylon | Fatigue resistance |
| Print high-strength gears | Nylon (PA6/PA12) | Self-lubricating, wear-resistant |
| Print impact-resistant parts | Polycarbonate | Highest impact resistance |
| Print food containers | PETG (food grade) | Food-safe certified, dishwasher-safe |
| Print miniatures/figurines | Standard Resin (SLA) | Highest detail, smooth surface |
| Print jewelry patterns | Castable Resin | Clean burnout for investment casting |
| Print architectural models | PLA or Wood-filled | Decorative, easy to post-process |
| Print supports | PVA (for PLA/PETG) or HIPS (for ABS) | Soluble, clean removal |

### Indoor vs Outdoor

| Material | Indoor | Outdoor (no direct sun) | Outdoor (direct sun) |
|----------|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| PLA | Excellent | Fair (6–12 months) | Poor (3–6 months) |
| ABS | Excellent | Fair (warping, yellowing) | Poor (UV degrades) |
| PETG | Excellent | Excellent | Good (2–5 years) |
| ASA | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent (5+ years) |
| Nylon | Excellent | Good | Good |
| PC | Excellent | Good | Good |

### Functional vs Decorative

| Use Case | Recommended | Alternative |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Decorative / display | PLA | Wood-filled, Silk PLA |

### Food Contact

Only PETG (food-grade variant) and PP are generally considered food-safe in FDM printing. Even then:
- Use a stainless steel nozzle (brass contains lead)
- Use a dedicated nozzle for food printing
- Layer lines can harbor bacteria
- PLA is compostable but NOT food-safe due to additives
- ABS/Nylon/PC should NOT be used for food contact

### Signs of Wet Filament
- Popping, crackling, hissing sounds during printing
- Bubbling or blobs on print surface
- Excessive stringing and ooze
- Rough surface finish
- Weak layer adhesion
- Steam visible at nozzle

### Recommended Storage Methods

1. **Dry box during printing** — For nylon, TPU, PETG, PC. Use airtight box with PTFE feed tube to printer. Add desiccant packs and digital hygrometer.

2. **Vacuum sealing** — Best for long-term storage. Store vacuum-sealed with silica gel. Can store for months to years.

3. **Airtight containers** — Large totes or bins with gasket seals. Add silica gel beads (indicating type changes color when saturated). Keep hygrometer inside to monitor.

4. **Filament dryers** — Dedicated devices (Sunlu S2/Filadryer, Polymaker PolyDryer, eSun eBox). Heat + circulate dry air. $40–100 for single-spool units. Can be used as dry boxes during printing.

5. **Homemade solutions** — 5-gallon bucket with gasket lid + desiccant + hygrometer. Or use printer's heated bed + cardboard box for drying.

### Drying Tips
- Over-drying some filaments (PETG especially) can make them brittle — dry only until no more weight loss
- Multiple short drying cycles are better than one long cycle for very wet filament
- Airflow dramatically improves drying efficiency
- After drying, seal filament immediately — it starts reabsorbing moisture from air within minutes
- In Iraq's dry climate (much of the year), PLA and PETG may need less drying than in humid regions

### Shelf Life
- PLA: 2–3 years in sealed bag with desiccant
- PETG: 1–2 years (more moisture sensitive)
- ABS: 2–3 years
- TPU: 1–2 years
- Nylon: 6–12 months unsealed (must dry before use)
- Resin: 12–18 months unopened (shake before use if settled)

## Safety Data

### Fumes / VOCs by Material

|----------|--------------|-------------------|------|--------------------|
| PLA | Low | Lactide, formaldehyde (trace) | Low | Open window, ventilated room |
| PETG | Low–Moderate | Cyclic siloxanes, acetone | Low | Ventilated room |
| ABS | High | Styrene, ethylbenzene | High | Enclosure + exhaust/filter required |
| ASA | High | Styrene, acrylates | High | Enclosure + exhaust/filter required |
| TPU | Low–Moderate | Siloxanes, isocyanates (trace) | Low–Moderate | Ventilated room |

### General Safety Recommendations

- **FDM with PLA/PETG:** Print in a room that's not your bedroom, open window, acceptable for occasional use.
- **FDM with ABS/ASA/Nylon/PC:** Sealed enclosure with activated carbon filtration, ideally vented to outside. Do not spend extended time in the print room during operation.
- **Resin printing:** Full PPE — nitrile gloves (5+ mil), splash-rated safety glasses, half-face respirator with organic vapor cartridges. External venting required. UV light hazard — protect skin and eyes.
- **All materials:** Keep printer away from flammable materials. Use smoke detector in room. Never print unattended overnight in a bedroom.

### Ultrafine Particles (UFPs)
- ABS generates highest UFP levels: ~2×10¹⁰ to ~9×10¹⁰ particles per minute
- PLA generates fewer UFPs but still measurable
- UFPs are small enough (20–100nm) to bypass lung filtration and enter bloodstream
- Practical mitigation: HEPA filter, ventilation, enclosure, distance

### Food Safety
- **PLA:** Not food-safe despite being plant-based (colorants, additives, layer line bacteria)
- **PETG:** Food-safe grades exist (e.g., PETG from Formfutura) — still need stainless steel nozzle + dedicated hotend
- **PP:** Food-safe, microwave-safe
- **ABS/Nylon/PC:** NOT food-safe — toxins can leach
- **General rule:** Do not use FDM prints for prolonged food contact. Coating with food-safe epoxy can help.

### Microplastics (When Sanding)
- Sanding 3D prints creates microplastic dust
- Wear N95 or better mask when sanding
- Wet sand to reduce airborne particles
- Work in ventilated area or use extraction

### Fire Risk
- FDM printers run at 200–300°C nozzle temperatures — fire hazard if thermistor fails (thermal runaway)
- Modern printers have thermal runaway protection — ensure yours does (Marlin firmware)
- Never leave printer unattended for very long prints without smoke alarm
- Loose connections are the most common cause of printer fires
- In Iraq's hot summers (50°C+ ambient), ensure printer electronics are not overheating
