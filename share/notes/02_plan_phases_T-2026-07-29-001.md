## Build ordering rationale

The default order is **A → B → C**.

1. **A first:** The taxonomy and resource index are the schema. They establish the canonical 12 kind IDs, the seven emerging IDs, spelling, preferred library names, official URLs, and license posture. Without A, every build guide could independently invent a stack name or kind number.
2. **B second:** Build guides consume the A index and add the implementation vocabulary that the do/don't, conversion, glossary, and references files must use. The 12 guides can be drafted in parallel inside B after the overview locks the mapping, but the overview must be reviewed before accepting guide files.
3. **C last:** The practice tables and appendix are cross-cutting consumers. Their reasons, library references, kind links, and correction entries are most reliable after A and B exist. The motion, license, and corrections documents may be drafted in parallel with B, but final cross-links and metrics are closed in C.

The chunks themselves are therefore sequential at the handoff level, with bounded internal parallelism: resource category files in A can be parallel after the index outline; individual guide files in B can be parallel after `03_build_guides.md` defines the template; C's `06–08` can be parallel with B only if the coder preserves the C exit gate. `04`, `05`, and the appendix remain after B because they cite the final guide IDs and vocabulary. `am-review` should review each completed chunk before the next chunk is accepted, even when the master uses adaptive parallel drafting.
