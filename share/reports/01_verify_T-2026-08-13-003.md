## Job 1 — the ask-first vs generate-first reconciliation

### What each side actually claims

- **Angle B (lines 13, 229-273, 387-396)** concludes: "The industry default is GENERATE-FIRST, REFINE-FOREVER, not ask-first." Its evidence is 12 closed tools surveyed; **9 ship generate-first by default**, 3 ship plan-first as opt-in (Devin, Base44 Discuss, Claude Code plan), and 0 ship ask-first as the default for app creation. The strongest single evidence cited is **Base44's Discuss mode at 0.3 credits/message vs full credits in Default mode** — i.e., the vendor frames ask-first as a credit-saving pause, not a feature. Angle B's own proposed synthesis (line 258): *"one bounded multi-choice survey at project start, then generate-first for the rest"*.

- **Angle E (lines 13-22, 380-388)** concludes: "Ask-first is the right default." Its evidence is empirical literature. Two citations carry the weight:
  - **[E-S2] Ambig-SWE (Vijayvargiya et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv:2502.13069)** — verified verbatim against the arXiv abstract: *"when models interact for underspecified inputs, they effectively obtain vital information from the user leading to significant improvements in performance, up to 74% over the non-interactive settings."* The paper exists, the number is exact, the venue (ICLR 2026) is correct.
  - **[E-S3] "Ask or Assume?" (Edwards & Schuster, 2026-03, arXiv:2603.26233v2)** — the 69.40% task-resolve rate is cited correctly; the "+19 percentage points" over the no-ask baseline is consistent with the paper's framing. I did not fetch the full text to double-check the exact pp figure, but the citation is structurally credible.

### Evidence weight, asymmetric

**The two sides are not symmetric.**

- Angle B's evidence is **observational across 12 production tools** in 2026 — every surviving closed competitor converged on generate-first. The base rate alone is a strong signal. Angle B itself flags the limitation: *"leaked system prompts on x1xhlol are [UNVERIFIED-CURRENCY]"* (line 392), so the *mechanism* claims are snapshots, not live prompts. But the *direction* (generate-first default) is verified through public docs (Base44, Claude Code, Bolt, Lovable, Replit) independently of any single leaked prompt.

- Angle E's evidence is **empirical but on a narrow population**. Ambig-SWE measures *bug-fix tasks on existing code* (SWE-Bench Verified underspecified variant), where the agent can ground clarifying questions against an existing repo. **Angle E itself flagged this caveat (R-7, line 438): "The 74% improvement from interaction ([S2]) was measured on SWE-bench, not on greenfield app generation. SWE-bench tasks are bug fixes with existing code; greenfield apps have no code to anchor the interaction. The +74% may not transfer."** This caveat is in the file but it does not constrain the final verdict.

- The Ghosh et al. 2026 paper [E-S8] — checklist beats interview on rubric scores (7.50 vs 6.67 vs 5.67) — is **a third independent data point** that pushes toward structured-restate-and-confirm, away from pure interview-style ask-first.

### Reconciled position

**Angle B's proposed synthesis ("one bounded ask-first turn at project start, then generate-first") IS supported by both bodies of evidence, not diplomatic split-the-difference.** Reasoning:

1. Every surviving closed tool has converged on generate-first for the *build loop* — this is convergent evolution under market pressure and is a load-bearing industry signal.
2. The Ambig-SWE +74% finding is real and demonstrates that clarifying questions help, but (a) it is on a different population, (b) Ghosh et al. show a structured checklist beats open-ended interview on a quality rubric, and (c) Angle E's own stop rule is bounded (10 questions cap, hard per-tier budget, coverage checklist) — which is precisely the "one bounded turn" pattern.
3. Angle E's intake protocol as designed (6 fixed axes + 0-4 adaptive, capped at 10, with defaults on every axis and a restate-and-confirm artifact) IS Angle B's synthesis in disguise. The structural content is compatible.

**The synthesis MUST adopt Angle B's framing explicitly**, with Angle E's content as the implementation. Specifically:

- Headline: "One bounded multi-choice intake at project start, then generate-first for the build loop" (Angle B's wording, line 258).
- Body: Angle E's 6-axis question bank, per-tier budgets, restate-and-confirm artifact, coverage checklist.
- Empirical anchor for *why one bounded turn* (not zero, not many): Ghosh et al. 2026 rubric numbers + Ambig-SWE transfer-caveated.
- Empirical anchor for *why not more*: a0.dev's 15-20 message / 80K char hard reset rule (Angle B-S17), Cursor's "keep going until resolved" anti-clarification stance (Angle B-S13), Claude Code's bounded Explore→Plan→Implement→Commit phases (Angle B-S23).

The current dossier has Angle E's "ask-first is the right default" verdict (line 380) AND Angle B's "the industry default is generate-first, not ask-first" verdict (line 13) sitting next to each other in the same synthesis. The synthesis writer must choose the framing above, not paper over the contradiction.

**Severity of leaving this unresolved: HIGH.** If the synthesis ships both verdicts and the user reads them in order, the user will not know what to build. (One HIGH finding, see F-1.)
