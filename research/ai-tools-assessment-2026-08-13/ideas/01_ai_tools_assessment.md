# Idea 1 — The AI Tools Assessment ($999 audit business)

> Source: Greg Isenberg (with Corey Ganon), "The $1,000/hour Solo AI business (Full Course)"
> Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dhbcVxYhWaQ
> Transcript timestamps: 3:46 (offer) – 25:01 (full flow), 26:20 (upsell menu)

## One-line pitch
A paid 45-minute "AI checkup" for small business owners: probe their biggest time drains, prescribe 3–7 off-the-shelf AI/SaaS tools that reclaim 5–10 hrs/week, and deliver a stupid-simple templated report — $999, guaranteed.

## Why this exists
Every SMB owner is 40–60 hrs/week in the weeds, afraid competitors are getting ahead on AI, and never takes the time to learn it. They don't need an automation agency and they can't talk to ChatGPT-bro consultants. They need a **doctor's prescription**: a short, clear, quantified answer to "what do I do about AI?" — with tools they can sign up for today. The prescription business has zero build risk: you never write code, never implement anything — you only recommend tools that already exist.

## The offer (exact structure)
- **What:** 45-min structured interview → 3–7 off-the-shelf AI tools prescribed → 5–10 hrs/week reclaimable
- **Guarantee:** if we can't find ≥5 hrs/week of AI opportunity, 100% money back → "the only thing you lose is 45 minutes"
- **Price:** $999 (foot in the door; LTV of an assessment client = $3–10K+)
- **Target ICP:** 2–20 employees, $500K–$5M revenue. Not solopreneurs, not enterprises
- **Stats from the episode:** ~15 sold in 2026; avg client reclaims 7 hrs/week; avg tool cost ~$60/mo; 50–60% buy implementation afterward

## Phase 1 — Discovery call (45 min)
- Zoom/Meet + an AI notetaker (Fathom default; Otter, Fireflies fine) — the transcript is the input to phase 2
- **Rule: probe only, never prescribe.** You will know the answer 30 seconds in — bite your tongue. First call is only for pulling out problems
- Question list (all of it):
  - "Walk me through your day yesterday."
  - "What do you tend to do in a typical business day?"
  - "What tasks do you dread doing?"
  - "Where does your work pile up?"
  - "What have you tried to automate in the past, and failed?"
  - Magic-wand question: *"If you could wave a magic wand and delete any process in your business, what would it be?"* (the #1 answer is almost always email)

## Phase 2 — AI analysis (the whole "AI expertise" you need)
- **Base prompt (≈1 paragraph):** "I just had a call with a business owner. Attached is the transcript of our conversation. Go on the internet and research off-the-shelf SaaS or AI tools that fix those pain points."
- Claude catches patterns you missed and finds tools for problems you thought unsolvable
- **The QA step (non-negotiable):** review every prescription. Claude will prescribe Salesforce for a 4-person landscaping company — sub in the small-business CRM. Horizontal AI optimizes for plausibility, not for the client
- **Skill-compounding loop:** feed past transcripts + finished reports back into the skill so it learns what "good" looks like. Expect 60–70% quality for the first 2–3 assessments (lots of substitutions), copy-paste quality by #4–6
- **Directories:** futurepedia.io (HubSpot-acquired) and theresanaiforthat.com — both industry-tagged, so "AI tools for realtors" is a filter, not a search

## Phase 3 — The report (the deliverable that's been iterated 12×)
Built in **Claude Design** (formerly Gamma), free plug-and-play template at **audittemplate.ai**. Slide stack:
1. **Title** — client, date, business type, primary focus
2. **Executive summary** — 1–2 pain points, main outcome, hours reclaimable (5–10, avg 7), primary focus = which of the **3 levers** the report pulls (effectiveness/money, efficiency/time, quality)
3. **Effort × Impact matrix** — report focuses on the top-left **quick wins** quadrant (high impact, low effort = off-the-shelf tools)
4. **Quick wins summary** — one pain → one tool per line ("5 hrs/week in email → SaneBox; hand-written meeting notes → Fathom")
5. **Recommended solutions** — the money slide (50% of review-call time): tool, pain solved, **cost, setup time, hours saved/week**. "It costs $10/mo, 30 min to set up, saves 2 hrs/week. Any questions?"
6. **4-day quick start plan** — ≤10 min/day, one tool per day → defeats the "where do I start?" freeze
7. **What comes after quick wins** — the **major projects** quadrant (high impact, high effort: agents, knowledge bases) — the upsell seed, on paper, before you've pitched anything
8. **Financial impact** — weekly hours × hourly rate − tool cost → always 4-figure, sometimes 5-figure monthly net ROI
9. **Next steps** — implement the plan, book the review call

**Design law:** "A confused mind doesn't buy, doesn't implement, doesn't get ROI, doesn't upsell." One pain → one tool per line. If the client has to think, the slide failed.

## Phase 4 — Review call (30 min)
- Email the report first so they've read it
- Screen-share walkthrough, ~50% of time on the recommended-solutions slide
- **The 3 closing questions:**
  1. "Of these recommendations, which is the most urgent for you?"
  2. "Do you want to do this yourself, or would you like my help implementing some of these?"
  3. "What's your timeline — are these pain points killing you, or can you live 60 more days?"
- Result: 50–60% of clients ask for implementation help → the upsell menu (see Idea 2 and the synthesis)

## Unit economics
- Fulfillment: 45-min call + ~1–2 hrs analysis + ~1–2 hrs report + 30-min call ≈ **5–6 hrs for $999** (~$165–$200/hr before any upsell)
- With 50% attach into $3–8K upsells, the assessment is effectively a paid qualification call
- Greg's scale idea: once the system is solid, spend $200 of each $999 on Meta ads → a self-liquidating funnel ("a beautiful vending machine")

## First-assessment checklist
1. Pick your wedge (geography *or* vertical — "the AI guy for X" beats "AI consultant")
2. Grab the template from audittemplate.ai, wire it into Claude Design
3. Build the phase-2 Claude skill (or just use the 1-paragraph prompt)
4. Sell 3 assessments to your warm network at a discount or free (feedback loop: transcripts + reports feed the skill)
5. Run the QA step on every prescription, always
6. Post every outcome publicly — your first "wins" are your first marketing
