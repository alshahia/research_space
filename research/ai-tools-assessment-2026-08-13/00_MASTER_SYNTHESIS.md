## TL;DR (90-second read)

Corey Ganon runs a **services-led, zero-code AI agency** built on one trip-wire offer and one retainer:

1. **The AI Tools Assessment ($999)** — a 45-min discovery call where you *only* probe (never prescribe), an AI pass (Claude skill) that maps pain points → 3–7 off-the-shelf tools, a templated report (free at audittemplate.ai), and a 30-min review call that closes 50–60% of clients into implementation work. Risk reversal: **"if we can't find 5 hrs/week of AI opportunity, 100% money back."** Average client reclaims 7 hrs/week for ~$60/mo in tools.
2. **The AI Concierge retainer ($1,200→$2,000/mo)** — two 45-min working sessions per month where you teach the client Claude skills via AOA (audit → optimize → automate), plus "unlimited" Voxer access that in practice receives ~1 message per client per 2.5 months. **Effective rate: ~$1,000/hour.** Corey went from idea to $8K MRR in 10 days (5 of 6 first prospects closed, price raised on every yes).
3. **Seven zero-capital, zero-audience client channels** — the strongest content in the episode: local AI meetup (SEO-like compounding), door knocking (30 doors → 5 meetings → 2 clients, per one listener's DM), LinkedIn DMs (probe, never pitch), free mini-audits to your existing network, agency/referral partners (insurance agents, accountants, real estate), AI office hours at coworking spaces (Dennis: 10 spaces → 2 yes → $175 in door-knocked gift cards → 11 attendees → 2 follow-ups), and build-in-public.

**The single biggest finding for this research space:** this video is the **services-led implementation playbook for the "Agent Readiness for Businesses" idea** ranked #1 in the Cloudflare synthesis — the paid audit wedge ("you're selling the screenshot") executed without any code or SaaS. And the endgame is visible in the same folder: **Zaher.AI is exactly what this service business looks like when it productizes into software.** The natural arc: assessment ($999) → concierge ($2K/mo) → vertical SaaS.

**What to steal even if you never start this business:** the "stupid simple output" principle ("a confused mind doesn't buy, doesn't implement, doesn't get ROI, doesn't upsell"), the 3-lever filter (money/time/quality), the effort×impact quick-wins framing, the $999-credit upsell trick, and the 90-day-win question.

## Why the offer converts (offer anatomy)

| Element | What he does | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk reversal** | "If we can't find ≥5 hrs/week, 100% refund" | Worst case for the client = 45 minutes of their time. Removes the buying risk entirely |
| **Quantified ROI** | Financial impact slide: weekly hours × hourly rate − ~$60/mo tool cost | "Always four figures, sometimes five figures, monthly net ROI" — the ROI is on the slide, not in the pitch |
| **Doctor metaphor** | "We're like a doctor writing a prescription" — prescribe, don't build | Low commitment ask ($999, not $10K); positions the upsell as the *treatment*, not a new pitch |
| **Stupid-simple deliverable** | 9-slide template, one pain → one tool per line, 4-day quick start plan (≤10 min/day) | Iterated 12×; every slide answers "what do I get, when, at what cost" |
| **Speed to value** | 4-day plan front-loads the benefit | Client feels the win before the review call is even booked |
| **Trip-wire philosophy** | "Your client is literally paying you to uncover opportunities for them to pay you more" | The assessment's job is to make the next offer *obvious*, not to be the business |

**Target ICP:** small business owners, 2–20 employees, $500K–$5M revenue. Not solopreneurs (no budget), not enterprises (too complex).

## The 4-phase fulfillment pipeline (fully reproducible)

| Phase | What happens | Tools | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Discovery call** | 45-min structured interview. Record with AI notetaker. **Probe only — no prescribing** ("I have to bite my tongue") | Zoom/Meet + Fathom (or Otter, Fireflies) | 45 min |
| **2. AI analysis** | Feed transcript to a Claude skill → pain points + researched tool candidates. QA step: substitute inappropriate tools (the "4-person landscaping co. doesn't need Salesforce" test) | Claude skill; directories: futurepedia.io (HubSpot-acquired), theresanaiforthat.com | ~1–2 hrs |
| **3. Report** | Templated, plug-and-play client deliverable | Claude Design (formerly Gamma); free template at audittemplate.ai | ~1–2 hrs |
| **4. Review call** | Email report first; 30-min screen-share walkthrough; **3 closing questions** → 50–60% take implementation | Zoom | 30 min |

**The phase-2 prompt (the entire "AI expertise" you need):** *"I just had a call with a business owner. Attached is the transcript. Research off-the-shelf SaaS or AI tools that fix their pain points."* Claude catches patterns you missed and finds tools for problems you thought unsolvable.

**The skill-compounding loop:** feed past transcripts + finished reports back into the skill. First 2–3 assessments land at 60–70% (expect substitutions); by assessments 4–6 the skill is at copy-paste quality. This is "eval context" done with real client data — no test suites needed.

**Why the QA step is the real moat:** a horizontal AI will prescribe Salesforce to a 4-person business. The human judgment call ("sub in a small-business CRM") is the difference between a doctor and a prescription dispenser. This is also why "you don't need to know AI" is *slightly* overstated — you need enough AI literacy to sanity-check prescriptions. See Honest pushback below.

## The report template (9 slides — free at audittemplate.ai)

1. **Title slide** — client name, date, business type, primary focus
2. **Executive summary** — 1–2 main pain points, main outcome, hours reclaimable (5–10; avg 7), **primary focus** (which of the 3 levers: effectiveness/money, efficiency/time, quality)
3. **Effort × Impact matrix** — top-left quadrant = **quick wins** (high impact, low effort). That's the whole report's focus
4. **Quick wins summary** — one-liners: "5 hrs/week in email → SaneBox" (his most-prescribed email tool), "hand-written meeting notes → Fathom"
5. **Recommended solutions** — the deep-dive slide, 50% of review-call time: tool name, pain it solves, **cost, setup time, hours saved/week**. "It costs $10/mo, 30 min to set up, saves you 2 hrs/week. Any questions?"
6. **4-day quick start plan** — ≤10 min/day, one tool per day; defeats the "where do I start?" freeze
7. **What comes after quick wins** — the **major projects** quadrant (high impact, high effort: dedicated agents, knowledge bases). This is the upsell seed planted on paper
8. **Financial impact** — weekly hours × hourly rate − monthly tool cost → 4–5 figure monthly net ROI
9. **Next steps** — implement the plan, book the review call

**Design rule:** one pain → one tool per line. If the client must think, the slide failed. Greg's own takeaway from this section: *"create an extremely stupid simple output."*

## The AI Concierge — why it's the $1,000/hr machine

- **Offer:** 2 × 45-min monthly Zoom working sessions (client shares screen; you build Claude skills together, teach Claude Co-work) + "unlimited Voxer, 12-business-hour response" (actual load: 3 of 5 clients messaged **once each** in 2.5 months — "virtually zero, very high perceived value").
- **First call = foundation, not build:** a Claude plugin walks the client through guided onboarding — connect tools, context files, global instructions, scheduled tasks, what a skill does. Every call after is 10× more effective.
- **Every session = AOA:** **A**udit ("show me how you do it today") → **O**ptimize ("13 steps → 7") → **A**utomate ("turn it into a Claude skill"). Rinse, repeat, for every workflow, for as long as they're a client.
- **Premium mechanics:** pre-engagement Google Form (mini-audit) that asks *"90 days from now, what would make this engagement feel like a win?"* — 4 of 5 clients hit their stated 90-day win by day ~60; then you remind them they exceeded it, and upsell again. A Notion one-pager holds the entire engagement + an inventory of accomplishments, emailed after every call — "that's what's staring them in the face that says keep paying."
- **Scarcity by design:** legitimately capped at 6 clients ("I'm taking one more client and I have two other calls this week"); Greg's landing-page variant: 4 spots @ $800 → $1,200 → $1,600 tiers.
- **Up-market forever:** when two clients churn, "I'm putting a $10,000/hour package together." There's always a more luxury experience for a specific ICP.

| 5 | **Agency / referral partners** | Low, sustained | Medium | Insurance agents, accountants, marketing agencies, coaches all get AI questions they can't answer. Offer referral fee; follow up every 2–3 weeks; co-branded workshops ("How to use AI to optimize your finances — by Corey + your tax firm") |
| 6 | **AI office hours at coworking spaces** | Low | Fast-ish | 1 hr/week on-site as free AI resource. Dennis: 10 spaces → 2 yes; door-knocked $175 in local gift cards as attendance carrot; 11 attendees, 2 follow-up calls, ~1 client |

**The persistence math:** "99% of people won't do these methods, and the ones who do quit after 3 days. Hang in 7 days and you're in the top 0.1%." The methods are low-competition precisely because they're uncomfortable.

## Frameworks worth stealing (regardless of business)

1. **The 3 levers** — any AI spend must pull effectiveness (make money), efficiency (save time), or quality (improve output). The report's "primary focus" is the dominant lever. This is also a great filter for *your own* AI product decisions.
2. **Effort × Impact matrix** — quick wins (report focus) vs. major projects (upsell seeds). Never put a high-effort item in a low-ticket deliverable; put it on the "what comes after" slide.
3. **AOA (audit → optimize → automate)** — automate *after* fixing the process; "throwing AI at a broken process makes it fall apart faster." This is the anti-hype ordering most AI sellers get backwards.
4. **Worst-case framing** — "at worst you learn 1–2 tools" converts fence-sitters in outreach and in reports.
5. **$999 credit trick** — price anchor games that make the client feel smart and you whole.
6. **Scarcity ladders & caps** — 4 spots @ $800 → $1,200 → $1,600; concierge capped at 6. Must be real or it backfires.
7. **90-day win question** — ask what success looks like *before* the engagement; deliver it early; show the inventory; upsell from the win.
8. **Stupid-simple output** — iterate the deliverable until the client can see their pain and their ROI in one glance.

## Honest pushback on the claims

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "You don't even need to know how to use AI" | ⚠️ **Overstated.** You need enough AI literacy to run the Claude skill, judge tool fits (the Salesforce substitution test), and walk a client through onboarding. The *entry bar is low* (hours, not years) — but not zero. Anyone who can't pass the QA step will deliver bad prescriptions and kill the 50% attach rate |
| "Maybe 5% of companies use AI beyond ChatGPT" | ❓ Unverifiable, self-serving — but directionally plausible; the TAM argument ("millions of businesses, every one needs AI") holds either way |
| "Blue ocean" | ✅ Directionally true at scale; **the real risk is local density of copycats**, not total market. His own counter to this — niche down by geography or vertical — is the correct answer and he says it explicitly |
| "Average client reclaims 7 hrs/week" | ⚠️ Self-reported; no measurement mechanism defined (the 5-hr guarantee is risk-reversal theater that almost never triggers, because time-finding is easy). Fine for marketing; don't build a business on audited numbers |
| "50–60% buy implementation" | ⚠️ Plausible given the credit trick and warm-audience mechanics; unverifiable without a client list. The mechanics are internally consistent |
| "The report template is the deliverable" | ❌ **The template is free** (audittemplate.ai — verified live). The moat is: sales motion, niche positioning, trust, and the QA judgment. The template is the *tool*, not the product |
| "$1,000/hour concierge" | ✅ **Math checks:** $1,500/mo ÷ 1.5 billable hrs = $1,000/hr; prep/Notion upkeep adds some hours but the ratio survives. Voxer reality (3 messages in 2.5 months) validates the structure |
| "AI meetup/office hours are cheap client sources" | ✅ Strong — lowest-competition, highest-trust channels. Correctly framed as SEO-like (slow compound) vs. door knocking (immediate) |
| "Door knocking is the best sales education" | ✅ True and underrated; the 30→5→2 listener data point is the whole pitch |
| Implicit: "this scales as a solo business" | ⚠️ It caps at ~6 concierge clients by design (his own scarcity). Scaling means either raising price up-market or productizing (Zaher shape). The episode never addresses this — the ceiling is the point of the concierge cap |

**Biggest unspoken risks:** (1) Claude-ecosystem dependence — the whole playbook assumes Claude skills/design/cowork stay stable and competitive; (2) directory quality — futurepedia/theresanaiforthat can be stale or SEO-gamed, which is exactly what the human QA step exists to catch; (3) the assessment→upsell funnel is only as strong as the founder's willingness to do uncomfortable outreach — the seven channels are all manual, and most people quit on day 3 (his own numbers).
